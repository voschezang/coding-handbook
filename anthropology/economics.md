# Economics

Economics is typically thought of as the study of exchange and markets. It tends to focus on highly specific phenomena and assumptions that are conviently chosen: efficient markets and rational, individualistic actors. The real world, however, is far less structured. This document discusses multiple types of economies and common misconceptions.

See also [macroeconomics](macroeconomics.md).

## Credit

**Credit** allow one to buy goods and services in exchange future compensation. It is effectively a formalization for reciprocal favors in *gift economies*. Various measures of accounting can be used. E.g. commodities such as precious metals, salt, grain, beads or cigarettes.

**Currency** (coinage) is an alternative for credit. It acts as a medium of exchange, unit of account and store of value. Unlike credit, it is anonymous and scalable. It can also be stolen more easily.

### Economic systems

Currency was around 600-500 BC, at the end of the iron age. Credit systems have been dated as early as 3000 BC, in ancient Kumer. In history there have been many different types of economies.

|                   | Qualitative Systems                                          | Quantitative Systems                                         |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Distributed**   | Gift economy. Favors with implicit (eventual) reciprocation. | Credit. E.g. settle tabs during harverst time.               |
| **Multicultural** | Barter. Immediate exchange                                   | Stockpile commodities. E.g. grain, salt, silver, beads, cigarets. |
| **Centralized**   | Planned economy. Enforced by government, temples, or elders. | Money. Market economy. Currency backed by government.        |

Bartering works well for strangers. Both parties attempt to scam the other, which is easier if they won't meet again after the deal.

Gift economies occor in many forms. Exchange within profit-driven organizations may be based on favours, rather than purely on formal rules and responsibilities.

**Market economies** are special cases. Although they are presented as distributed systems, all currencies lead back to a state. States put significant pressure to ensure the value of their currencies follows their needs. Counterfeiting is prosecuted harshly and the state forces vendors to accept thier currency as payment. When states fail to control their currency it can become worthless. Currencies can be bought and sold as a commodity too, which complicates the categorization further.

## Market Efficiency

Efficient markets are a central theme in economics. However, they are contingent on a myriad of factors. It requires that economic actors are rationalm have acces to perfect information, and are able to trade. This is rarely the case. Consider

- [Bounded rationality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bounded_rationality). Decision making is based on assumptions rather than perfect information. Or based on limitations or fear. Only in hindsight is the optimal decision clear.
- Cryonism and nepotism. People tend to favour those within their class, clan or family. Realtors may give better deals to their friends.

## Capitalism

Economics is often equated with capitalism. Reality is more nuanced.

- **Markets** are about exchanging of goods and services. Sell a commodity, to be able to buy another commodity. Trades are driven by need.
- **Capitalism** is about the accomulation of wealth. Invest in some commodity, to be able to sell it at a profit. Trades are driven by greed.

Capitalism tends include three parties.

- **Investors** (bankers). They provide capital, calculated with mathematical precision.
- **Entrepeneurs** or adventurers. They take on debt to be able to start business ventures or military endaveours.
- **States** facilitate markets and enforce payment of debt. With or without bankrupcy laws.

Capitalist dynamics tend to conceal accountability. Investors tend to focus on accounting rather than outcomes. Entrepeneurs do hold accountability for their actions, but they are pressured by the weight of their debt. In history entrepeneurs have taken wildly unethical decisions. Accountability is even more obfuscated when corporations are involved. Managers are bound by company policy to make ruthless decisions, acting according to their role instead of their own morals.

In the past, such dynamics where dampened by the prohibition of usury by the Christian and Islamic churches. The dissolution with monasteries during the protestant reformation is correlated with capitalist growth.
