# Macroeconomics

Fundamentals of economic systems. See also [economics](economics.md).

**Inflation**. Inflated prices make money less valuable.

**Interest** is paid as compensation for loans. Households receive interest on savings accounts and governments pay interest on bonds.

## Printing Money

Traditionally, banks would lend out money at interest. Modern banks have effectively the ability to create electronic money through loans. Central banks may even create money by buying assets (e.g. bonds). It is expected that most loans are paid back. However, a fraction of loans is expected to default. The premium compensates for this. When too many loans default, it causes problems. Trust in (central) banks or will diminish.

When a debtor cannot pay, there can legal (or violent) consequences. A state or patron steps in.

### Monetary Policy

**Central banks** can control interest rates:

- Increasing interest rates make *saving* more lucrative for households, incentivizing them to consume less.
- Increasing interest rates makes *loans* (investments) more expensive, which constrains the growth of bank-generated money, risking deflation.
- *Decreasing* interest rates makes loans (investments) cheaper, which will increase the money supply, risking inflation.

**Bonds** are "old" loans. These loans are freely tradable. Interest is paid over the duration of the bond. 

- Bond *yields* are the annual percentage of the effective rate of return of the bond, based on the price, coupon and interest rate. The price is inversely related to the yield.
- Bond prices are inversely related to interest rates. Increasing interest rates make new bonds more lucrative, thereby deflating prices of older bonds.

- Conversely, decreasing interest rates makes bonds more expensive.

Central banks can buy and sell bonds. **Quantitative easing** involves buying bonds.

- Buying bonds increases the demand of bonds, making bonds more expensive. Consequently, the government can borrow more cheaply, reducing the need to pay premiums for bonds, eventually reducing interest rates on new bonds.
- Buying bonds increases the money supply indirectly. This could cause either inflation, inflated asset prices or more production.
